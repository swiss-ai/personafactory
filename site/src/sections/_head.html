PersonaFactoryStylistic User Prompt Augmentation with Synthetic Persona Bank

Bich Ngoc Doan1*, Hanna Yukhymenko2*, Imanol Schlag2

1EPFL   2ETH Zurich   *Equal contribution

Paper (soon) Code Dataset

Abstract.Large language models increasingly replace real people in the AI development pipeline, as they are used to generate training dialogues at scale and to drive alignment and evaluation. However, out-of-the-box models produce monolingual and stylistically flat texts as synthetic user prompts, failing to replicate the real user-AI communication mechanics. We present PersonaFactory, an end-to-end pipeline that generates detailed user personas and uses them to augment existing user prompts from their point of view, producing a bank of over 740000 structured synthetic personas detailing their background and communication behaviors, and a corpus of roughly 1.24 million persona-styled synthetic conversations spanning 85 languages. We evaluate the effect of personal features on style adoption and intent preservation, using an LLM-judge and heuristic metrics to assess augmentation fidelity together with the downstream impact on assistant persona control through training data. Results show that our structured, attribute-based style representation enables per-axis control and better intent preservation at comparable style fidelity than free-text persona descriptions used in existing methods. A human expert evaluation found the augmented conversations plausible, with favorable ratings for intent preservation, style naturalness, and multilingual authenticity. Finally, PersonaFactory conversations achieve comparable performance to original training data on capability benchmarks, are preferred on real user prompts at equal reply length, and adapt their reply style more closely to users without explicit steering.

Findings

Finding 1

Structured style beats a free-text description

60% vs 33% preferred

Rewriter, persona and length budget are held fixed. The judge prefers the rewrite with per-axis style keys nearly twice as often as the free-text one, at the same 92% intent preservation, and three judges from outside the pipeline agree. When the free text carries the same facts at the same length, the two tie on style, and the structured form keeps the request more often and reads more naturally.

Finding 2

Experts find the rewrites plausible

59% judged human by raters

In a preliminary study with 11 expert submissions in nine languages, intent, style and plausibility get a median rating of 4 out of 5. Raters took 59% of verified rewrites for human-written and told them from real messages with 55% accuracy. LLM judges took 83–89% for human-written and did no better than chance. A trained classifier can still tell a rewrite from the real message it came from.

Finding 3

Models trained on it keep capability and adapt to the user

0.392 vs 0.125 emoji mirroring, released vs original data

Apertus-v1.5-8B trained on the released conversations is on par with training on the original ones on capability benchmarks. It scores 1.8 points higher on IFEval and 3.1 lower on GSM8K, the one benchmark where it stays behind. Two judges from outside the pipeline prefer its replies to real user prompts in 54.6–57.5% of cases at equal length, and it mirrors the user's emoji use and formality more closely.
